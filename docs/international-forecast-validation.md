# International forecast validation: Block 11

Block 11 is the first international spread block for the 2026 Bundibugyo virus outbreak. It was pinned on 2026-10-07, its window runs from 2026-10-08 to 2026-11-04, and reports published up to 2026-11-07 count when they state an in-window confirmation date. Every number below is regenerated by `lovs/forecast/pins_block11.py` from `data/international-events.json` (reports through 2026-10-07) and `data/importation-reference-class.json`.

## Why the block looks the way it does

International events in this outbreak are rare: three first detections outside the DRC in 21 weeks (Uganda on 2026-05-15, France on 2026-06-24, Kenya on 2026-10-06). One block can resolve only a few outcomes, so a single block cannot settle which forecasting method is better. The block is therefore the first of a monthly series with fixed methods. The outcomes accumulate, and a decision rule registered now is applied once enough of them exist.

The questions test five things the programme would otherwise assume:

1. Whether the exportation hazard is stationary or has fallen as the response matured.
2. Whether exports go to DRC land neighbours or to countries reached by air (two of the three first detections were not neighbours).
3. Whether Uganda re-seeds. Uganda reported no new confirmed case after 2026-06-21, while the DRC's cumulative confirmed count rose roughly eightfold (1,048 to 8,603), and the Kenya patient crossed Uganda on 2026-10-02.
4. How often a recognised importation is followed by local transmission.
5. Which of the two methods should be the forecaster of record.

## Methods

**Counting unit.** A period is seven days counted back from the window opening (2026-10-08) to the period holding the outbreak declaration (2026-05-15): 21 periods, from 2026-05-14 to 2026-10-07. A period is positive when at least one qualifying report was first published in it. Counting periods rather than days keeps the estimate from depending on how often a source happened to be captured.

**Hazard methods.** Each estimates a per-period probability with a Jeffreys prior, (k + 0.5) / (n + 1), where k of n periods were positive, and converts it to the probability of at least one event in the window's four periods: 1 - (1 - p)^4.

- Recent method: the last 13 periods (2026-07-09 to 2026-10-07). It is the forecaster of record for this block.
- Stationary method: all 21 periods. It is priced beside the recent method as the comparison.

The recent method is the record because the outbreak's own data show the cross-border hazard changing: Uganda's quiet run and the end of its outbreak (local transmission declared over on 2026-07-28; the outbreak declared over on 2026-08-27). This choice was made before any outcome. The decision rule below can reverse it.

**Destination split.** The neighbour and non-neighbour questions multiply the per-period probability by a Jeffreys-smoothed share of first detections in DRC land neighbours, taken from the method's own window. All 21 periods hold three first detections, one in a neighbour (Uganda), so the share is 0.375. The last 13 periods hold one, in a non-neighbour (Kenya), so the share is 0.25.

**Reference class.** The Kenya question asks whether a recognised importation is followed by a further case. It is priced from documented travel-related importations into countries without ongoing transmission, 2000 to 2026, under inclusion rules written before the search (recorded in the reference-class file). Of 13 episodes, 5 were followed by locally acquired confirmed cases. With a Jeffreys prior this gives 0.393.

## Registered prices

| Question | Recent (record) | Stationary (comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| Any country outside the affected set reports a first case | 0.3645 | 0.5000 |
| A DRC land neighbour not yet affected reports a first case | 0.1029 | 0.2181 |
| Any other new country reports a first case | 0.2847 | 0.3422 |
| Uganda reports a new confirmed case | 0.1354 | 0.6836 |
| Kenya reports a further confirmed case | 0.3929 (reference class) | n/a |

The affected set at registration is the DRC, Uganda, France, Germany, the Netherlands, the United States and Kenya. That is every country that had reported or treated a confirmed case, whatever the route, so no affected-country list artefact or medical evacuation can resolve a question. A planned medical evacuation of an already-confirmed patient never counts.

The methods ignore context the registry does not encode. The clearest example is the Kenya patient's transit through Uganda, which the Uganda prices do not reflect. That is deliberate: a fixed method is what makes the series comparable from month to month.

## Decision rule and scoring

- **Primary endpoint:** the mean paired Brier difference (stationary minus recent) over resolved hazard questions, pooled across international blocks priced by the same two methods.
- **Decision rule:** once at least 12 paired hazard questions have resolved, the stationary method becomes the forecaster of record if the pooled mean difference is below zero and it wins at least 8 of 12 non-tied questions (ceil(8 × k / 12) of k). Otherwise the recent method stays. A difference of exactly zero is not a win. The rule is evaluated by `lovs/forecast_scoring.paired_block_decision` with question ids as units.
- **Fewer than 12 pairs:** the comparison is descriptive only. This block alone holds four pairs, and its three new-country questions share one hazard and are not independent.
- **Void rule:** a question is void for every method when a matching report was first published after the source cutoff and before the window opened, or when an in-window report states an earlier confirmation date.
- **Silence:** never resolves a pin NO. A NO needs a coverage review through 2026-11-04, recorded in the registry on or after 2026-11-07.

## Limitations

- **Report dates.** Uganda's middle reports are dated by the first capture in the programme's source corpus, not the authority's own publication date. The seven-day periods absorb most of that error.
- **Reference class.** The class is small and mixed across viruses and decades. Four of its 13 indexes were returning health workers in high-income settings, all without onward transmission. Recoding one episode moves the price by about eight points.
- **Missing importations.** Importations never diagnosed anywhere are missing from every count here. That biases the exportation hazard down, and the reference class in an unknown direction.
