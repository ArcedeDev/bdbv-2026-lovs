# International forecast validation: Block 11

Block 11 is the first international spread block for the 2026 Bundibugyo virus outbreak. It was pinned on 2026-10-07, and its window runs from 2026-10-08 to 2026-11-04. Reports published up to 2026-11-07 also count when they state an in-window confirmation date. Every number below is regenerated by `lovs/forecast/pins_block11.py` from two inputs: the nine substrate reports in `data/international-events.json` and the 13 frozen episodes in `data/importation-reference-class.json`. The substrate was reviewed at 2026-10-07T00:24Z and covers whole days through 2026-10-06.

## Why the block looks the way it does

International events in this outbreak are rare: three first detections outside the DRC in 21 weeks (Uganda on 2026-05-15, France on 2026-06-24, Kenya on 2026-10-06). One block can resolve only a few outcomes, so a single block cannot settle which forecasting method is better. The block is therefore the first of a monthly series with fixed methods. The outcomes accumulate, and a decision rule registered now is applied once enough of them exist.

The questions test five things the programme would otherwise assume:

1. Whether the exportation hazard is stationary or has fallen as the response matured.
2. Whether exports go to DRC land neighbours or to countries reached by air (two of the three first detections were not neighbours).
3. Whether Uganda re-seeds. Uganda reported no new confirmed case after 2026-06-21, while the DRC's cumulative confirmed count rose roughly eightfold (1,048 to 8,603), and the Kenya patient crossed Uganda on 2026-10-02.
4. How often a recognised importation is followed by locally acquired cases.
5. Which of the two methods should be the forecaster of record.

## Methods

**Counting unit.** A period is seven days counted back from the window opening (2026-10-08) to the period holding the outbreak declaration (2026-05-15): 21 periods, from 2026-05-14 to 2026-10-07. A period is positive when at least one qualifying report was first published in it. Counting periods rather than days keeps the estimate from depending on how often a source happened to be captured.

**Hazard methods.** Each estimates a per-period probability with a Jeffreys prior, (k + 0.5) / (n + 1), where k of n periods were positive, and converts it to the probability of at least one event in the window's four periods: 1 - (1 - p)^4.

- Recent method: the last 13 periods (2026-07-09 to 2026-10-07). It is the forecaster of record for this block.
- Stationary method: all 21 periods. It is priced beside the recent method as the comparison.

**How the window and the record were chosen.** The 13-period window and the choice of the recent method as record were made during design, after both methods' prices had been computed. They were not fixed blind. The reason given is that the outbreak's own data show the cross-border hazard changing: Uganda's quiet run and the end of its outbreak (local transmission declared over on 2026-07-28; the outbreak declared over on 2026-08-27).

Because the recent method prices lower on all four hazard questions, a quiet window favours it on every pair, and a busy one favours the stationary method. That asymmetry is why the decision rule pools across monthly blocks rather than judging this one.

**Destination split.** The neighbour and non-neighbour questions multiply the per-period probability by a Jeffreys-smoothed share of first detections in DRC land neighbours, taken from the method's own window. All 21 periods hold three first detections, one in a neighbour (Uganda), so the share is 0.375. The last 13 periods hold one, in a non-neighbour (Kenya), so the share is 0.25.

**Reference class.** The Kenya question asks whether Kenya reports a further case acquired in Kenya. It is priced from documented travel-related importations into countries without ongoing transmission, 2000 to 2026, coded under the research brief written before the search (2026-10-06T23:09Z). The brief listed Dallas and New York (2014), and Kayes and the Bamako cluster in Mali (2014), as separate episodes, and defined the outcome per importation. Of 13 episodes, 5 were followed by locally acquired confirmed cases. With a Jeffreys prior this gives 0.393.

At review, with both candidate prices visible, a reading that merges concurrent arrivals in one country was considered. It gives 6 of 12 and 0.500. It was rejected because it reverses the brief's own labelling, and it is reported under Sensitivity.

**What counts.**
- **Confirmed case:** a country counts as affected when its own authority, or WHO on its behalf, has reported a laboratory-confirmed case. At registration that is the DRC, Uganda, France and Kenya.
- **Evacuations:** Germany and the Netherlands have only treated patients confirmed in the DRC, and the United States appears on ECDC's list because of its evacuated citizens. A case any of them confirms in a traveller would be its first. A case in a person moved by a planned medical evacuation never counts, whether confirmed before or after the transfer.
- **Authorities:** WHO, ECDC, US CDC, Africa CDC, or the country's ministry of health or national public health agency. A press report alone does not count.
- **Which country:** a case counts for the country whose authority reports its laboratory confirmation.
- **Kenya:** the case must be stated by the authority to have been acquired in Kenya. A new importation does not count, and the imported case confirmed on 2026-10-06 does not count.

The methods ignore context the registry does not encode. The clearest example is the Kenya patient's transit through Uganda, which the Uganda prices do not reflect. That is deliberate: a fixed method is what makes the series comparable from month to month.

## Decision rule and scoring

- **Primary endpoint:** the mean paired Brier difference (stationary minus recent) over resolved hazard questions, pooled across international blocks priced by the same two methods.
- **Decision rule:** applied once, to exactly the first 12 paired hazard questions to resolve, ordered by resolution date and then ledger order. It is evaluated by `lovs/forecast_scoring.paired_block_decision(need=8, of_metrics=12)`, with question ids as units.
- **Switch condition:** the stationary method becomes the forecaster of record if the mean difference is below zero and it is better on at least 8 of the 12. A question on which both methods score the same counts as not better.
- **Before 12 pairs resolve:** the comparison is descriptive only. This block holds four pairs, and its three new-country questions are nested, so they are not independent.
- **Void rule:** a question is void for every method in either of two cases:
  - a matching report outside the frozen substrate was first published before the window opened;
  - a matching report published by the end of the grace states a confirmation date before the window.

  The Kenya question is therefore conditional on no locally acquired case being reported before 2026-10-08.
- **Before the push:** the sources are checked once more. A report found then is never added to the substrate or used to reprice; it voids the questions it would decide.
- **Registration receipt:** the resolver scores nothing without a receipt, and voids every pin whose receipt is later than 2026-10-07T23:59:59Z.
- **Silence:** never resolves a pin NO. No pin is scored without a coverage review naming its sources, covering every day through 2026-11-07 and made after it.
- **Finality:** retractions and facts stated after a report (local acquisition, a confirmation date) count only when published by 2026-11-07, so nothing published after that can change a resolution.

- **Status on 2026-10-20: a resolving report is recorded, and the score waits for the registered review.** On 20 October Nigeria's Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare announced Nigeria's first confirmed case, an imported case in a traveller from Uganda, confirmed by laboratory tests that day (registry report `nga-2026-10-20`). Nigeria is neither a DRC land neighbour nor one of the four countries affected at registration, so the report meets the new-country and new-non-neighbour questions and not the new-neighbour question; it raises no Uganda count, because a case counts for the country whose authority reports its laboratory confirmation. WHO's Regional Office for Africa states that a laboratory test confirmed Bundibugyo virus, and Uganda's Ministry of Health states, as preliminary information, that the traveller had been in the eastern DRC and transited Uganda without symptoms. These questions are scored only at the registered coverage review, covering every day through 2026-11-07 and made after it; a retraction, or an authority statement that the case is not part of this outbreak, before then would change the outcome. If the outcome stands, the scores will be: new country 0.3645 for the method of record (Brier 0.404) and 0.5000 for the comparison method (Brier 0.250); new non-neighbour 0.2847 (Brier 0.512) and 0.3422 (Brier 0.433). The three new-country questions are nested and not independent, and one outcome does not decide between the methods; the decision rule waits for 12 resolved pairs.

## Limitations

- **Report dates.** Uganda's middle reports are dated by the first capture in the programme's source corpus, not the authority's own publication date. The seven-day periods absorb most of that error.
- **Reference class.** The class is small and mixed across viruses and decades. Recoding one episode moves the price by about seven points. Its outcome is local transmission attributed to the importation, while the Kenya question counts any locally acquired case in Kenya, including one from a later importation. The question is slightly broader than the class, which biases the price low.
- **Missing importations.** Importations never diagnosed anywhere are missing from every count here. That biases the exportation hazard down.
- **Curator judgement.** The curator records what an authority states. Local acquisition is recorded only when stated, on the report or by a later dated attribution. A discarded case is withdrawn only by a dated retraction of the whole report. Where an authority is silent, the pin leans toward NO rather than YES.

## Addendum: Kenya escape forecast and incubation evidence

By 17 October Kenya had seven confirmed cases: the imported first case and six linked cases. All six were contacts of the first case and were in quarantine when their symptoms began. More cases among quarantined contacts are expected and are contained, so the programme registered a forecast of the risk that matters next: will Kenya report at least one laboratory-confirmed case, confirmed 2026-10-18 to 2026-11-14 and reported by 2026-11-17, that was not under follow-up as a known contact (quarantine, isolation or active monitoring) when its symptoms began and is not a new importation? An unlisted contact, a listed contact never traced, a contact whose illness began after follow-up ended, and a case the authority links to a Kenyan case or describes as local transmission all count. New importations are not forecast: with the response to further importations likely heightened after the first case, their rate is too uncertain to score. The scored artifact is `data/case-conditioned/kenya-2026-10-18-escape-forecast-v3.json` (from `lovs/forecast/kenya_escape_v3.py`); the incubation evidence is `data/case-conditioned/kenya-2026-10-17-incubation.json` (from `lovs/forecast/kenya_incubation.py`).

- **Incubation evidence.** The six linked cases are the first Bundibugyo cases since 2012 with published exposure windows and onsets. The health workers were exposed on 3 and 4 October and the relatives between 3 and 5 October, and onsets fell on 13 to 17 October, with none earlier among contacts in quarantine. These data favour a longer incubation than the programme prior (mean 6.7 days): a 10-day mean fits about 120 times better under the main reading (about 22 to 200 times across the alternative readings), and the decided prior (mean 7.4 days) about 4 times better. Combined with the earlier Bundibugyo evidence, the mean is about 8 days. On its own the Kenya cluster sets a lower bound, not an upper one, because onsets seen only to 17 October cannot rule out a very long incubation. Only case 2's onset date is published; the others are inferred from confirmation dates. Six cases from one cluster are thin evidence.
- **Is 21-day follow-up long enough?** In the forecast's incubation reading (which allows onsets two days before the inferred windows, because an early test can be negative, and allows that two relatives were infected by case 2 in quarantine), the mean is 7.75 days and 1.0% of incubations exceed 21 days. A contact of the first case who is infected but not yet ill on 17 October has about a 6 to 9% chance that illness begins after the 21 days end, around 25 and 26 October. This is evidence for review, not a recommendation on follow-up length.
- **Scored value.** 0.166 (80% interval 0.058 to 0.313, parameter uncertainty inside the model only), the curve value for 17 October. Moving one judgement input at a time gives 0.13 to 0.28. The curve falls each day with no qualifying report: 0.081 on 24 October, 0.031 on 31 October and 0.005 on 7 November.
- **How it is built: two independent estimates.** The key unknown is how much of the first case's transmission reached people who were never listed.
  - The Kenya cluster works as a natural experiment: exposure dates are known and every listed contact was checked daily. All six secondary cases came from about 31 close contacts (relatives and health workers), and none from about 99 listed contacts without close contact, allowing for those still incubating. This agrees with Kikwit 1995, where no household member without physical contact was infected (0 of 78). Built from this mechanism, the unlisted share is about 2%, and the forecast alone would be 0.134.
  - Past recognised importations with secondary cases (Nigeria, Dallas and Madrid, 2014), weighted equally, imply a much higher and very uncertain share; alone they give 0.281.
  - Each model draw uses one estimate with equal probability. The absence of any case outside follow-up by 17 October counts against the draws with a high share, so after conditioning the past episodes carry 20% of the weight. The two estimates disagree more than tenfold before conditioning, and the artifact states this.
- **Detection lag.** Onset to report for a case outside follow-up is drawn from 2 to 5 days: Kenya's own floor (case 2 was confirmed a day after onset under daily checks, plus a day to seek care) and, because no onset-to-isolation delay is published for this epidemic, the 2014 West Africa mean as the ceiling.
- **Evidence that was sought and not found.** Whether any of Uganda's five 2026 secondary cases was under follow-up when symptoms began is not published, so Uganda does not enter the reference class (entering it at 0 to 3 of 5 gives 0.165). Nigeria's count outside follow-up is documented for one case and inferred for two, so the rule draws it from 3 to 19.
- **Benchmark.** The share of reference-class episodes with any case outside follow-up, 0.625, is a loose upper reference, not a fair test: it counts whole episodes and ignores Kenya's silence to 17 October. The model is not calibrated against past outcomes.
- **What a YES means.** A YES is not an outbreak in Kenya. It would mean that one case was not on a contact list or not under follow-up when it fell ill. The forecast says nothing about the quality of Kenya's response and is the programme's own estimate, not an official one.
- **Resolution.** An authority report (Kenya's Ministry of Health, WHO, Africa CDC, ECDC or US CDC), with a coverage review naming its sources made between 2026-11-21 and 2026-12-05. Follow-up by any public-health authority counts, including another country's and the workplace monitoring of health workers caring for cases. A case the authorities call a listed contact, with no statement of its status at onset, does not count; a case they describe neither as a listed contact nor as under follow-up at onset counts. A case is a new importation, and does not count, when an authority reports a stay, in the 21 days before onset, in an area with a confirmed case in the 42 days before that onset. Anything else unclear is decided by a reviewer other than the forecast's author, named by role in the review. The result is published whatever the outcome.
